\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The evidence on the controlling block is associational: the block is measured before the announcement and cannot be its consequence, but controllers with small stakes run different firms and groups, and the block is the stake as the parent reports it, not the family's cash-flow right through the pyramid. The signal predicts the disposal and the return, and early disposal and the signal share their association with the return, the pattern a chain from intention to disposal to loss would produce; it is also the pattern that would arise if filings that read as disposals are written by parents already in decline for reasons the controls miss, and the controls, industry effects, benchmarks and selection model make that reading harder to sustain without ruling it out. The return horizon is one year, where the early disposals fall; the association vanishes against a single matched firm and holds against matched portfolios, and we report both. The outcome label pools a listing, a majority sale and a dilution below 99.5 percent, so a small third-party stake counts as a disposal; Table S2 shows the headline results with the definition-sensitive labels flipped. The reform tests rest on the equity spin-off as an untreated form and on \AppraisalPrices{} appraisal prices and \ProtPostN{} protection blocks; they show the direction of each channel on a short record. The signal is produced by language models, and Section~\ref{sec:validity} reports where it is weaker---small and unlisted parents, filings before 2009---as well as where it is stable; the outcome labels are read from the parents' reports partly by a model as well, cross-checked and stress-tested but not hand-coded in full. A codebook and a stratified sample of filings for a blind human reading, the one test that would separate what a 2026 model knows about how such filings end from what the filing says, are in the replication archive; that reading has not been run.

Korean controlling owners who later disposed of a carved-out subsidiary wrote division filings that differed from those written by controllers who kept it, in ways a careful reader could detect and beyond anything a form's purpose categories record; the difference was larger where the controller's stake was smaller. Minority investors received the filings that signalled a disposal more favourably at the announcement and lost over the following year as the disposals arrived. Whether a remedy that acts at the disposal would do better than one that acts at the division is the question these results leave open. The 2022 reform changed what the filings said and how they were received, gave dissenters a put that is near the money, and separated intentions where it induced the controller to bind itself; within the horizon we can observe, it did not change what the filings forecast.
