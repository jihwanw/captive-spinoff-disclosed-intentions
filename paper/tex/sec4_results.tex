\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The filing predicts the disposal}\label{sec:gate1}

The disposal signal separates the subsidiaries that were later disposed of from those retained (Table~\ref{tab:gate1}; the measure is the AUC of equation~\ref{eq:auc}, where 0.5 is chance and 1 a perfect ordering). Across the \NGateOne{} clean-label divisions of 2001--2025 the two-model signal reaches \AUCPooledLLM{}. The financial baseline reaches \AUCPooledFin{}; the difference of \DeltaPooled{} has a paired bootstrap interval of [\DeltaPooledLo, \DeltaPooledHi]. The signal is stronger in the recent period (\AUCNewLLM{} for 2017--2025 against \AUCNewFin{} for the financial model) than in the earlier ones. A financial model trained on the 2001--2016 divisions and scored on 2017--2025 reaches \AUCTemporalFin{} against the signal's \AUCTemporalLLM{} on the same test set, so the gap is not an artefact of fitting within period. The filing says more about what the controller will do than the balance sheets of the two companies it creates. Figure~\ref{fig:roc} draws the receiver operating characteristic curves behind these numbers.

Three filings show what the models read. Doosan's filing of June 2021 for the carve-out of its forklift business stated that, once the division was complete, the parent would sell its entire stake in the new company to an affiliate, Doosan Bobcat; the two-model signal is \ExDoosanScore{} and the subsidiary was \ExDoosanOutcome{}. CJ ENM's filing of July 2020 for the carve-out of its streaming service listed, among the division's purposes, ``attracting outside investment, strategic alliances, technological cooperation and the sale of shares where necessary''; the signal is \ExTvingScore{} and the subsidiary was \ExTvingOutcome{}. DB HiTek's filing of May 2023 stated that the parent would hold 100 percent of the new company and wrote into both companies' charters that any listing within five years of the division would require a special resolution of the parent's shareholders; the signal is \ExDBScore{} and the subsidiary was \ExDBOutcome{}. The sentences the models quote are the ones that state, or decline to state, who will own the new company.

The anonymized condition answers the recall objection. For the 2001--2016 divisions, where the models most often reported recognising the company, the two-model signal falls from \AUCOldIdent{} with the names to \AUCOldAnon{} without them; the paired bootstrap interval of the gap, [\GapOldLo, \GapOldHi], includes zero. For 2017--2026 it falls from \AUCNewIdent{} to \AUCNewAnon. The filings Model~A said it recognised are the ones it scores best, but it scores them equally well when the names are masked (\AUCClaudeFlaggedAnonOld{} against \AUCClaudeFlaggedIdentOld{}). Those filings are easier to read, and the masking shows that the names were not what made them so. The return results below have been repeated with the anonymized signal in place of the identified one. The Spearman correlation with the one-year return is \SpearmanAnon{} ($p$ = \SpearmanAnonP), the within-year rank coefficient with the full controls \RankAnonCoef{} ($p$ = \RankAnonP), and the early-disposal AUC \AUCEarlyAnon{} on the \NAUCEarlyAnon{} events with an anonymized score and a recorded timing, against \AUCEarlyPooled{} on the \NAUCEarly{} events with the identified score; the two samples differ, so the last pair is not a paired comparison (Supporting Information).

The signal is also sharper about the disposals that matter for the one-year return. Against the retained subsidiaries, it orders the subsidiaries disposed of within one fiscal year of the division with an AUC of \AUCEarlyPooled{} (\AUCEarlyNew{} in 2017--2026), and those disposed of two or more years later with \AUCLateVsNever. What the filing tells a reader most reliably is whether the controller already has a buyer, an investor or a listing in mind.

\subsection{The market's reaction and the year after}\label{sec:market}

The announcement reaction does not fall with the disposal signal; it rises (Table~\ref{tab:returns_by_score}, which sorts the listed parents into quintiles of the signal). The slope of the three-day abnormal return on the signal is \AnnCarSlope{} (standard error \AnnCarSlopeSE{}, $p$ = \AnnCarSlopeP{}) with year fixed effects, so a filing that reads as a certain disposal is received \AnnCarSlopePP{} percentage points more favourably than one that reads as a certain retention. The return over the following year falls with the signal. The Spearman correlation between the signal and the one-year return is \SpearmanScoreBhar{} ($p$ = \SpearmanScoreBharP) across \NScoreOneY{} parents, and \SpearmanWithinYear{} ($p$ = \SpearmanWithinYearP) when both are ranked within the announcement year. The difference between the top and bottom quintiles is \QQWithinYear{} percentage points in the within-year sort and \QQDiff{} points in the pooled sort, with wide intervals because the returns are heavy-tailed; at the median it is \QQMedianDiff{} points (\QFiveMedian{} against \QOneMedian{} percent; Mann--Whitney $p$ = \QQMWP). The calendar-time tests point the same way without reaching conventional significance. A daily portfolio long the top tercile and short the bottom tercile earns \CTHiLo{} percent a year (Newey--West $t$ = \CTHiLoT), and its monthly five-factor alpha is \FFHiLo{} percent a month ($t$ = \FFHiLoT).

The rank-based evidence is clear and the mean-based evidence suggestive, as a right-skewed return distribution produces when the typical event loses and a few events gain a great deal. Across all \NScoreOneY{} parents the mean one-year market-adjusted return is \DriftMean{} percent (bootstrap interval [\DriftLo, \DriftHi]), while the median is \DriftMedian{} percent and \DriftNeg{} percent of parents trail their index; the five-factor alpha of the equal-weighted calendar-time portfolio is \FFAllAlpha{} percent a month ($t$ = \FFAllT).

The mean one-year return of parents whose subsidiary was disposed of differs from that of parents that retained it by \DispRetDiff{} percentage points (Mann--Whitney $p$ = \DispRetMWP; Welch $p$ = \DispRetWelchP), but the average conceals the timing. The \EarlyN{} parents whose subsidiary changed hands within one fiscal year of the division earned \EarlyMean{} percent on average and \EarlyMedian{} percent at the median, against \NeverMean{} and \NeverMedian{} percent for the \NeverN{} parents that retained; the difference in means is \EarlyNeverDiff{} points (Mann--Whitney $p$ = \EarlyNeverMWP; bootstrap interval [\EarlyNeverLo, \EarlyNeverHi]). Parents whose disposal came two or more years later earned \LateMean{} percent, indistinguishable from the retainers ($p$ = \LateNeverP). The loss is where \citeA{kang2025} located it, in the disposal, and within the limits of a report-year label it arrives when the disposal does.

The association between the signal and the year's return is not the parent's condition in disguise, as far as the controls we can measure reach (Table~\ref{tab:controls}); a troubled parent might both write a filing that reads as a disposal and go on to underperform. The dependent variable in the first row of equation~\ref{eq:main} is the parent's percentile rank of the one-year return within its announcement year, which is immune to the skew. The coefficient on the signal is \RankSpecOneCoef{} with year fixed effects alone, \RankSpecTwoCoef{} when size, book-to-market, the six-month pre-announcement return and the market are added, and \RankSpecThreeCoef{} when the new company's relative size, the surviving company's debt ratio and the stated listing plan are added as well (HC1 $p$ = \RankSpecThreeP; $p$ \RankSpecThreeClP{} clustered by year); across the three pooled specifications it lies between \RankScoreCoefMin{} and \RankScoreCoefMax{}. A filing that reads as a certain disposal rather than a certain retention thus places the parent \RankSpecThreePctl{} percentiles lower in its year's return ranking. The coefficient is \RankSpecThreeBCoef{} in 2001--2016 alone ($p$ = \RankSpecThreeBP), \RankSpecThreeCCoef{} in 2017--2026 alone ($p$ = \RankSpecThreeCP) and \RankSpecFourCoef{} in 2017--2026 with the parent's own return on assets, debt ratio and size added ($p$ = \RankSpecFourP). The coefficients on the mean return in the second row are negative in five of the six specifications and imprecise in all. The slope of the announcement return on the signal in the fourth row stays positive in every specification; it is significant in the pooled sample and in 2001--2016, and not in 2017--2026 alone.

The signal's association with the return runs through the realised early disposal (Table~\ref{tab:realized_controls}). The coefficient on early disposal in the one-year return is \JointEarlyCoef{} with the same controls ($p$ = \JointEarlyP), and when early disposal and the signal enter together the signal's coefficient is \JointScoreCoef{} ($p$ = \JointScoreP): once we know that the subsidiary changed hands within the year, what the filing said adds nothing to the return. This is a conditional association rather than a mediation estimate: the signal's information about the return is the information it carries about the early disposal.

\subsection{The controlling owner's stake}\label{sec:block}

Parents with small controlling blocks wrote the filings that read most as disposals, disposed most often and earned least (Tables~\ref{tab:block_terciles} and~\ref{tab:block_regressions}). Sorted into terciles of the block held before the announcement, the parents in the lowest tercile (blocks up to \LowBlockMax{} percent of common shares) filed divisions that read more strongly as disposals. They disposed of the subsidiary more often (\LowBlockDisp{} percent against \HighBlockDisp{} percent in the highest tercile, blocks of \HighBlockMin{} percent and above) and disposed of it early more often (\LowBlockEarly{} against \HighBlockEarly{} percent). And they earned less over the following year: a median one-year return of \LowBlockMedian{} percent against \HighBlockMedian{} percent (Mann--Whitney $p$ \LowHighMWP), a mean of \LowBlockMean{} against \HighBlockMean{} percent.

Figure~\ref{fig:block} shows the four gradients side by side. The regressions put numbers on the chain. The disposal signal falls with the block (\ScoreOnBlock{} per unit of the block, HC1 $p$ = \ScoreOnBlockP, $p$ = \ScoreOnBlockClP{} clustered by year): controllers with smaller stakes write filings that a reader scores as more likely to end in a disposal. The realised outcomes move the same way with less precision: the coefficient of the disposal indicator on the block is \BlockOnDisposed{} (standard error \BlockOnDisposedSE) and that of the early-disposal indicator \BlockOnEarlyAlone{} (\BlockOnEarlyAloneSE) with year effects alone. Once the signal is added, the block's coefficient on early disposal is \BlockOnEarlyWithScore{} (\BlockOnEarlyWithScoreSE) while the signal's is \ScoreOnEarlyWithBlock{}. What the block tells us about the disposal is already in the filing. The parent's rank in its year's return rises with the block (\RankOnBlock{} per unit, $p$ = \RankOnBlockP), and the announcement reaction does not vary with it (\BlockOnCar{} per unit, standard error \BlockOnCarSE).

The two sources of loss overlap rather than add (last two rows of Table~\ref{tab:block_regressions}), which estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interaction}
y_i = \alpha_{t(i)} + \beta_1\,\mathrm{Early}_i + \beta_2\,\mathrm{Low}_i + \beta_3\,\mathrm{Early}_i\times\mathrm{Low}_i + \boldsymbol{\gamma}'\mathbf{x}_i + \varepsilon_i,
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{Early}_i$ the early-disposal indicator and $\mathrm{Low}_i$ equal to one when the block is below the sample median. In parents with a large block the coefficient of early disposal is \EarlyCoefBhar{} on the one-year return and \EarlyCoefRank{} on the within-year rank; that of a small block is \LowBlockCoefBhar{} and \LowBlockCoefRank{} regardless of disposal; and the interaction terms, \InterCoefBhar{} ($p$ = \InterPBhar) and \InterCoefRank{} ($p$ = \InterPRank), offset most of the early-disposal effect in small-block parents, for whom the net coefficient of early disposal on the rank is \EarlyInLowBlockRank. In small-block parents the underperformance is present before any disposal is recorded; in large-block parents it appears with the recorded disposal.

This is the pattern the principal--principal frame predicts and the one \citeA{kang2025} found with a different sample and ownership measure: the minority shareholders who bear the loss are the ones whose controller had the least to lose. It is an association; controllers with small stakes may run different businesses, and we have no instrument for the stake.

\subsection{The 2022 reform}\label{sec:reform}

The revised form changed what the filings say (Table~\ref{tab:reform} and Figure~\ref{fig:reform}, which compares the filings received from 2017 to September 2022 with the \NPostFilings{} read by the models among the \NPostAll{} received from October 2022 onward). A shareholder-protection statement appears in \ProtectPre{} percent of the earlier filings (one filing, dated 23 September 2022, between the package and the form) and in \ProtectPost{} percent of the later ones; a statement about the new company's listing plans appears in \ListingPre{} and \ListingPost{} percent. Captive spin-offs became rarer, from \FilingsPerYearPre{} filings a year before the cutoff to \FilingsPerYearPost{} after it. The announcement reaction grew, from \CarPre{} percent to \CarPost{} percent (Mann--Whitney $p$ \CarPrePostP; \NPost{} post-cutoff parents with prices), so that a captive spin-off announced under the new form is received as good news. What the filing signals about the disposal is unchanged within what the sample can detect: the mean disposal signal is \ScorePre{} before and \ScorePost{} after ($p$ = \ScorePrePostP). Nor, over the horizon we can see, does what the controllers did: among divisions old enough to have two fiscal years of annual reports, \EarlyPre{} percent disposed within a year before the reform and \EarlyPost{} percent after (Fisher $p$ = \EarlyPrePostP). The comparison rests on \NPostOutcomes{} post-reform outcomes and could detect, at conventional power, only a change of about \MDEEarlyPP{} percentage points in that share, the minimum detectable difference
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mde}
\delta = (z_{0.975} + z_{0.80})\sqrt{\bar p(1-\bar p)\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{n_{\mathrm{pre}}} + \tfrac{1}{n_{\mathrm{post}}}\Bigr)}
\end{equation}
at the pre-reform share $\bar p$; the score comparison could detect a change of about \MDEScore{} on the unit scale (pre-reform standard deviation \ScoreSDPre), so neither null is tight. The share eventually disposed of is lower after the reform, but the later divisions' disposals have had less time to appear, and we do not rest on that comparison.

Two cautions belong here. The post-reform sample is small (\NPost{} announcement returns, and fewer mature outcomes), and there is no group of divisions the reform did not touch, so the differences are before-and-after descriptions rather than estimates of the reform's effect. And the appraisal right took effect only in late December 2022, so the first post-reform quarter had the disclosure rule and the listing review (in force from 28 September 2022) without the appraisal right; nothing in the design separates the three measures.
